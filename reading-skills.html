Day 1 · Guided practice

Finding the Main Idea

What to learn: The main idea is the central point the author wants you to understand. Supporting details explain or illustrate it, but are not the entire message.

Strategy: After reading, ask “What does every paragraph contribute to?” Choose the answer broad enough to cover the passage, but not so broad that it loses the author's focus.

Worked example: A passage describes how a town built a seed library, stored seeds carefully, and invited residents to return new seeds. Its main idea is how community participation helps preserve plant varieties—not simply how to label seed envelopes.
